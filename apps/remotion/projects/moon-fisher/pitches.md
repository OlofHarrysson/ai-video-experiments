# Pitches

Four premises pitched on 2026-09-29 for a calm short with no dialogue, in pixel art drawn in code. Olof chose The Moon Fisher. The others stay available for later films. Return to [the project](README.md).

## The Moon Fisher (chosen)

*An old fisherman who can't catch anything hooks the moon's reflection and pulls the moon into his boat.*

- **Setup:** a still night sea, a tiny boat, the fisherman and his cat. The moon's reflection wobbles beside his line. Nothing bites, and he dozes off.
- **Turn:** the line catches on the reflection and he pulls up the moon itself. The sky goes black and the sea goes flat. The only light left is the moon in his bucket, lighting his face from below. Then it starts to fade, like a fish out of water.
- **Payoff:** he tips it back into the sea. It sinks, then rises into the sky, and the sea fills with silver light. A fish leaps into the boat and the cat gets it. Feeling: gentle wonder, then a smile.
- **Why pixel art and code:** the moon is the only light, so one value, where the moon is, recolors every asset. Shimmering water and reflections are classic palette-cycling effects. There are only a handful of assets to perfect, and the boat can rock to a slow lullaby.

## Snail Mail

*A snail climbs a garden wall to deliver a birthday card and arrives exactly one year later.*

- **Setup:** a birthday party in a window box at the top of a wall. At the bottom, a snail with a card bigger than itself starts climbing as the balloons come down.
- **Turn:** the seasons pass as it climbs: summer heat, autumn leaves, then winter snow, which it sleeps through under the card.
- **Payoff:** spring. It reaches the top just as next year's balloons go up, right on time. Its friend hands it a thank-you card to take back, and the snail looks down the wall. Feeling: amused warmth.
- **Why pixel art and code:** one set, recolored for each season by swapping palettes. Ivy drawn by code, and weather as particles. One continuous upward tilt tracks the climb and uses the whole vertical frame. A snail acts with two eye stalks, which small sprites do well. The score can be one tune arranged four ways, one per season.

## Stargazer

*A kid with a telescope can't find a single star over the city, until the power goes out.*

- **Setup:** a tall apartment block with every window lit, under an empty orange sky. On a balcony, a kid points a telescope at nothing.
- **Turn:** a flicker, then a blackout and silence. Candles appear, window by window.
- **Payoff:** the neighbors step onto their balconies and look up. The camera tilts past the roof into the Milky Way. Feeling: quiet awe, shared with the neighbors. It is loosely based on an often-told story that people in Los Angeles reported a strange silver cloud during the 1994 earthquake blackout.
- **Why pixel art and code:** the building is a grid of tiny window scenes that switch on the beat. The film is one journey of light, from electric to candle to starlight, done with palettes and dithering. The Milky Way can be generated by code, and the music can cut out at the blackout.
- **Cost:** many tiny people to animate.

## The Window Fox

*On a night train, a kid draws a fox in the fogged-up window, and it leaps out to race the train.*

- **Setup:** a night train through snow. A sleepy kid breathes on the glass and draws a fox.
- **Turn:** the fox jumps out and runs alongside through a forest, over a frozen lake and past a sleeping town. The kid follows it from window to window.
- **Payoff:** dawn at the coast. The fog clears and the drawing fades. Then a real fox watches the train from the platform. Feeling: wonder that it might have been real.
- **Why pixel art and code:** layered backgrounds that scroll at different speeds. The clack of the wheels sets the beat. A white line-drawn fox against the pixel colors gives a built-in contrast of styles.
- **Cost:** the most backgrounds of the four, and it suits horizontal 16:9 better.
