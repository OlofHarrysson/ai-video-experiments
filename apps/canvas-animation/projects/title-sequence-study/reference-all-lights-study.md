## Direct visual evidence

Inspected 20 overview frames, 38 offset frames and 28 dense-window frames, with exact source timestamps and frame indices retained in the local review manifests. These are visual observations from stills and dense frame sequences, not claims of full-speed audiovisual review.

- 88.5 s: LED dots; one letter has dim pins while others are illuminated.
- 89.5 / 98.5 s: horizontal slats and displaced scan rows.
- 83.5 / 90.5 s: misregistered thin contours, long rules, double outlines.
- 94.5 / 99.5 s: angular italic construction, triangle shards and long thin strokes.
- 96.333–96.583 s: receding outlines stretch toward a vanishing point; depth and foreground size change before a cut.
- 97 / 107 s: bulb letters and ornamental sign structures, including curls, border rails and tiny bulbs.
- 109.333–109.583 s: ornate serif title, decorative arcs and dense lamps alternate with full black frames. This is not a smooth camera move.
- 110.833–111.083 s: soft segmented rings and lettering switch to a dotted version, with black frames interspersed.
- 114.833–115.083 s: a small winged tube word switches to giant vertical hairlines; the lower half is sliced sideways, then another outlined word overlays it, then a script card appears.
- 111.5 s: large block glyph behind a thin outline word.
- 113 s: overlapping repeated words in multiple treatments.
- Quiet and tiny cards coexist with extreme crop and dense ornament. Most cards use only one or two colours and little or no glow.

## Working interpretation

Variety comes from letter construction, material, composition, scale and exposure. Do not conflate every difference with a new font. Registered material swapping from the earlier Night Fever study remains useful, but the new source also contains spatial effects (depth changes, split slats, rings). Motion should belong to the construction; avoid applying the same camera animation to every style.
