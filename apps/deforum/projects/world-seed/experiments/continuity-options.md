# Crisp detail and persistent focal objects

2026-10-12. Review of preserved experiment reports and configurations, prompted by Olof's playback feedback. No new inference or playback screening was performed in this audit. The original film remains selected. Proposals below are not implemented or validated recipes.

## Human target

In the recent tidal comparison, Olof finds starting noise 0.25 really bad, 0.6 acceptable and 0.72 better in some respects; 0.85 changes large objects too much. Small flicker and incidental objects changing are acceptable. A major object such as the moon disappearing is not. Continue exploring concrete material descriptions, but judge both rich individual paintings and persistence across the moving sequence. These preferences do not select 0.72 as a universal default.

The tidal prompt already mentions the distant ringed planet throughout: it was not dropped from the travelling text. The model is still free to reinterpret its size, location and number. Clearer object wording is a cheap hypothesis, not a structural constraint.

Its model and extra RGB-noise mechanism differ from Krea. Older scheduler `denoise` fractions are not numerically interchangeable with today's explicit starting sigma.

## Interpreting steps and CFG

More sampling steps subdivide the denoising path; they do not append a restoration operation after a finished image. Krea Turbo is an eight-step distilled model, and its official full-generation recipe disables extra classifier-free guidance. Our three-interval partial repaint is an experimental adaptation, not an official recurrent-video recipe. More steps remain testable, but our evidence gives little reason to expect them alone to restore fine relief.

Krea's CLI calls the disabled setting `cfg=0`; ordinary ComfyUI calls the equivalent conditional-only prediction `CFG=1`. Lowering ComfyUI below 1 changes the mixture toward the negative branch; it is not directly a previous-image preservation control. Our audition used zeroed negative conditioning. Sources rechecked 2026-10-12: [official model instructions](https://github.com/krea-ai/krea-2), [Krea sampler](https://github.com/krea-ai/krea-2/blob/main/sampling.py), [our pinned Comfy sampler](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/blob/12d5279438bfefc058a269eae805ceab6047777f/comfy/samplers.py).

## Most useful next comparisons

1. **Protect a moving focal form while allowing richer repainting elsewhere.** Start with the tidal planet and then an architectural focal form in a different world. Compare global 0.6, global 0.72 and 0.72 with an object-aligned sampling mask, keeping prompt, seeds, camera, steps and CFG1 matched. The mask must travel with the same spatial transform as the painting and be released when a transformation is intended. Start with a clear protected/editable distinction; ordinary gray mask values are not calibrated local sigma values. This addresses object persistence directly, but could repeat the earlier softness or create boundary artifacts. Do not treat it as an established fix or add pixel copyback silently: Olof previously deferred that mechanism over artifact concerns. Reject it if it looks pasted, frozen or smeared.
2. **A separate surface-detail pass.** I found no completed matched Krea experiment that creates a base recurrent chain and then applies a distinct low-strength detail pass to its saved paintings. Test a few saved paintings first; only continue if relief improves. Refine a short sequence outside the recurrent feedback, before identical RIFE finishing, so the extra pass cannot accumulate across future generation inputs. This isolates finishing from story/structure generation. It cannot reliably restore a moon already missing from the base chain, and independently regenerated texture may shimmer. Repainting the entire image twice is not automatically refinement. Judge the unrefined and refined versions together before adding resolution or another model.
3. **Higher native resolution, separately.** The prompt study's 2016×1344 engraved city is a strong still reference, but has not been animated. A matched short 1536×1024 versus 2016×1344 sequence could test whether the larger grid retains useful fine structure in motion. Resolution also changes the generated trajectory; this is not an identical-content pixel comparison, and it does not directly constrain object identity.

The first proposal has the closest connection to the disappearing-moon complaint, but the earlier mask failure makes a short feasibility screen essential. Comfy supports sampling masks and a separate experimental differential-denoising mechanism; source availability is not proof of a good Krea result. With only three sampling intervals, differential activation also has coarse timing. [Pinned sampling-mask implementation](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/blob/12d5279438bfefc058a269eae805ceab6047777f/comfy/samplers.py#L570), [differential diffusion](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/blob/12d5279438bfefc058a269eae805ceab6047777f/comfy_extras/nodes_differential_diffusion.py).

Identity/edit reference conditioning is a further option if a mask cannot allow natural evolution. The existing [reference research](../../../../../docs/research/krea-reference-continuity.md) documents candidate adapters, but their current compatibility would need a fresh audit before implementation. Do not equate the failed style-reference probe with an identity-preservation test.

For any follow-up, hold scene-specific material wording constant across sampling arms. Require the main moon/building to retain count, recognizable silhouette and a camera-consistent path; allow small incidental changes. Inspect native paintings for relief and disappearance, then all displayed frames around two suspect transitions for softness or doubling. A sharper still, lower pixel difference, or successful interpolation is not evidence of a better film. No new render batch is started by this review.
