## Human target

In the recent tidal comparison, Olof finds starting noise 0.25 really bad, 0.6 acceptable and 0.72 better in some respects; 0.85 changes large objects too much. Small flicker and incidental objects changing are acceptable. A major object such as the moon disappearing is not. Continue exploring concrete material descriptions, but judge both rich individual paintings and persistence across the moving sequence. These preferences do not select 0.72 as a universal default.

The tidal prompt already mentions the distant ringed planet throughout: it was not dropped from the travelling text. The model is still free to reinterpret its size, location and number. Clearer object wording is a cheap hypothesis, not a structural constraint.

## Interpreting steps and CFG

More sampling steps subdivide the denoising path; they do not append a restoration operation after a finished image. Krea Turbo is an eight-step distilled model, and its official full-generation recipe disables extra classifier-free guidance. Our three-interval partial repaint is an experimental adaptation, not an official recurrent-video recipe. More steps remain testable, but our evidence gives little reason to expect them alone to restore fine relief.

Krea's CLI calls the disabled setting `cfg=0`; ordinary ComfyUI calls the equivalent conditional-only prediction `CFG=1`. Lowering ComfyUI below 1 changes the mixture toward the negative branch; it is not directly a previous-image preservation control. Our audition used zeroed negative conditioning. Sources rechecked 2026-10-12: [official model instructions](https://github.com/krea-ai/krea-2), [Krea sampler](https://github.com/krea-ai/krea-2/blob/main/sampling.py), [our pinned Comfy sampler](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/blob/12d5279438bfefc058a269eae805ceab6047777f/comfy/samplers.py).

## Direction after clarification — 2026-10-12

Olof rules out masks because of artifact concerns. The previously proposed moving-object mask comparison is declined and must not be started. He is interested in exploring a separate detail pass and asks why its output would not become the next generation's input. This is clarification and direction, not selection of a validated two-pass recipe.

**Noise within a repaint versus across the video.** The current `ps-tidal-specific-n072` config holds starting noise at 0.72 across its recurrent repaints. Within each repaint, the executed recipe builder scales the standard sigma list to approximately **0.72 → 0.615413 → 0.373081 → 0**: three Euler updates. Fresh random noise is introduced at the start of the next repaint; ordinary Euler with the current default zero churn does not inject a fresh noise field at every sampling update. The initial opening uses its separate full-generation recipe. Sources: [schedule builder](../../../src/deforum_lab/rendering/schedules.py), [repaint graph](../../../src/deforum_lab/rendering/graphs.py), [case config](configs/ps-tidal-specific-n072.json), [pinned Euler implementation](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/blob/12d5279438bfefc058a269eae805ceab6047777f/comfy/k_diffusion/sampling.py#L169).

1. **Noise schedule first.** Compare the existing 0.72 → 0.615413 → 0.373081 → 0 path with 0.72 → 0.512844 → 0.310901 → 0, alongside the current 0.6 baseline. This holds the 0.72 initial mixture and three Euler updates fixed while testing intermediate levels. See the [noise/reference research and staged experiment design](../../../../../docs/research/reference-conditioned-repainting.md).
2. **Additional image-reference conditioning, without masks.** The previous painting can remain the sampler's starting canvas and also be supplied through a trained editing adapter as a clean visual reference. The proposed benefit is giving the model information about what should survive even after noise has disturbed the starting canvas. There is no pixel lock or guarantee of object count. The earlier failed style-reference probe is not evidence about an identity/edit-trained adapter. The community [Identity Edit author card](https://huggingface.co/conradlocke/krea2-identity-edit/blob/89e9e7a09ee2e5c9331e952063d79b1b8a703280/README.md), rechecked 2026-10-12, claims instruction-based preservation but acknowledges changes outside the requested edit. It emphasizes people/identity; surreal-world recurrence is unvalidated. Our [reference research](../../../../../docs/research/krea-reference-continuity.md) records the candidate's graph requirements. This is a possible later study, not an installed or proven fix.
3. **Deferred detail pass, with both feedback policies treated as hypotheses.** First establish whether a second gentle pass actually improves a few saved paintings. If useful, compare the original sequence with two short variants: finish its saved paintings outside feedback, or refine each new painting before warping that refined output into the next generation. Keeping refinements outside feedback is an experimental control: it isolates their direct visual effect and preserves the exact original base chain. Feeding them back is legitimate and may carry richer structure forward, but may also accumulate invented texture, contrast changes or geometry errors. Neither policy is known to be better. The feedback variant requires regenerating subsequent base paintings, so later comparisons assess complete trajectories. Keep camera, prompt, seeds, primary noise and finishing matched where possible. Do not call extra sampling intervals in one pass a two-pass test.
4. **Separate later resolution study.** Current moving paintings are 1536×1024 (about 1.57 million pixels). The still study's 2016×1344 city uses about 2.71 million pixels, 72% more. The proposal is to generate and repaint the sequence at that larger size, then optionally reduce the delivery size. It is not merely exporting the existing video larger. More spatial room may help tiny engravings and edges, but costs more computation and does not ensure persistence or sharpness. Changing the latent grid changes composition and trajectory even with the same seed. The larger city is a still reference, not proof of moving-image improvement.

Olof subsequently prioritizes noise and reference conditioning, with research before experiments; refinement follows those studies. Masks remain ruled out. The [research update](../../../../../docs/research/reference-conditioned-repainting.md) distinguishes stochastic sampling, target initialization and clean reference conditioning, revisits both earlier reference studies, and specifies the proposed sequence. For any follow-up, hold scene-specific material wording constant across sampling arms. Require the main moon/building to retain count, recognizable silhouette and a camera-consistent path; allow small incidental changes. Inspect native paintings for relief and disappearance, then all displayed frames around suspect transitions for softness or doubling. No new render batch was started during this clarification.
