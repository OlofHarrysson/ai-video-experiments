## Visual review

Cream paper, loose black outlines, cobalt-blue coats and marks, with very limited warm accents. Open space makes the drawings readable. A blue-coated figure recurs at a relatively small scale while calendars, commuters, tables, clocks and symbols change around them. The person provides a visual anchor even as poses and details vary.

The imagery has a consistent vocabulary rather than a single physically stable scene. Scribbles become structures; outlines remain lively and imperfect. Apparent viewpoint shifts and reframing support changing subjects, but these samples do not prove a particular 3D camera path. At 14.25s the calendar/commuter composition remains; by 14.375s it has become trains. Treat that as an abrupt scene change, not evidence of a perfectly continuous morph. Exact editing versus prompt-switch mechanisms remain unknown.

## What to reproduce

Keep the paper ground, limited palette, line-weight variation, generous empty space, a recurring human-scale anchor and a short sequence of recognizable visual metaphors. A good still is insufficient if the next frames become muddy or fill the empty space with unnecessary detail. This is a useful second study: one calendar motif changing into commuters/trains over 5–8s. Avoid generated readable text in the initial test. Exact character continuity and narrative transitions are additional challenges, not solved by copying a color palette.

Evidence: 24 overview samples, 12 one-second detail samples and eight closely spaced samples around the calendar/train change. Reviews label decoded timestamps; sampled-frame study cannot establish full playback rhythm or audio synchronization.
