# First-pass screening

Assistant and independent-agent observations. Source-painting inspection, camera-only previews and delivered-frame checks are distinct evidence. Sampled frames cannot establish the complete rhythm or flicker quality of normal-speed playback; Olof's taste judgment remains open.

## Observed progression

- The opening's lamp and red ribbon lead toward the copper turbine. The lamp passes offscreen while the turbine grows into a close-up.
- The approach crosses beneath the solid hub, with ivory blades becoming enormous cloud ridges. It is a visual transformation, not proof of a physically consistent flight through machinery.
- A diagonal bank and pan open a cloud canyon. Several red forms persist; they connect scenes by color and shape rather than verified single-object identity.
- The storm becomes a giant curling canopy. Its shadow reaches the white plain, and a red kite emerges beneath it.
- The selected ending branches from source 1560, before the next attempted escape introduces a second kite. Its first repaint attempt became patterned and bird-like; the replacement is screened in the production notebook.

## Motion evidence

Camera-only previews were inspected before generation. Two storm-exit plans ran into obstructing clouds and were rejected. The first pullback reversal crowded the kite against the upper edge; a quicker reversal preserved its framing. Pullback previews reflect the borders of one source painting and cannot predict newly generated scenery.

A fixed 7×5 coordinate-grid diagnostic over selected planned intervals gives a mean displacement of 0.0894 image heights per delivered second versus 0.0498 for Night Orchard, a 1.80 ratio; median displacement is 0.0806 versus 0.0421. Peaks are similar. This supports the intended increase in sustained geometric movement, without quantifying perceived motion or visual quality.

The final join and the kite/storm/rocks remain coherent in those windows; spars, tail shapes and small rocks still redraw.
