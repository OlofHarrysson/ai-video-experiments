## What the approaches taught us

| Approach | Procedure | Useful result | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prewritten | Freeze the complete bird/cage/forest arc before the opening. Rewind and revise toward the same intention. | A simple central symbol gives a coherent theme. The revised door is easier to read. | Stronger instructions add another bird instead of moving the original. More literal detail can complicate identity. |
| Improvised | Freeze only the opening. Inspect nose/moon alignment, clock doorway and closed whale eye before choosing each continuation. | The station clock returns as a giant destination. Reframing the actual doorway gives a distinct arrival. | A retrospective explanation is not proof of depicted action. Timestamp decisions. |
| Hybrid | Keep “a vulnerable light finds refuge,” but accept the wave becoming shelter around the boat. Extend the observed beacon into shared lights. | Intention survives while unexpected forms guide the route. Lantern → beacon → constellation supplies an ending. | Arrival, guidance and release of lights remain suggested rather than fully enacted. |

My working recommendation is **hybrid planning**: choose an emotional direction and one or two recognizable motifs, plan the next short passage, then adapt its exact route to the painting. This recommendation comes from these examples; the subjects differ, so it does not establish universal superiority. The strongest improvised ending also needed deliberate geometric planning and an edit.

The independent reader saw neutral paintings without plots. It recognized liberation in the bird film, a journey through time/dream in the whale film, and harbor light extending into the sky. It favored the revised whale arrival and constellation as clearer endings, without claiming reliable literal action or agency. See [independent review](independent-review.md), [main visual review](quality-review.md), and the [timestamped decisions](DECISIONS.md).

## Controls and limits

Recurrent Krea Turbo, CFG1, three Euler intervals, independent recorded noise and descriptive intermediate prompts. Every repaint starts from the encoded **warped previous painting**. Source: 24 fps and half-second repaints. Delivery: 1.5× faster, 24 fps, three paintings per displayed second. RIFE 4.25 scale 1 never feeds back. Final seven frames are actual spatial warps after the last retained painting. No audio.

P2 changes text/noise timing, H3 isolates motion, and P3/P4 isolate a small noise comparison. The wider story methods have different subjects. No new model, CFG, sampler or repaint-frequency winner is claimed.

I5 measures the actual window center and solves a translation that lands it under the composed mapping. [Framing receipt](doorway-framing.json) and [motion-only preview](../../exports/storytelling-lab-v001/review/doorway-framing.jpg) preserve the calculation. This is fixed-image geometry, not automatic semantic tracking through later repaints.

Still unresolved: abrupt local redraws, RIFE ghosts, reflected borders, late flattening and identity during action. Editing at the strongest painting does not solve recurrent degradation. Explicit position prompts and higher noise did not solve bird relocation. A future bounded experiment could focus on one clearly visible interaction.
