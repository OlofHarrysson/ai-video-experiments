## Intent and human feedback

Olof called the previous two studies another improvement and preferred Negative Space. He liked the basslines more than some other instruments, and some timbres and the beat still did not fit his taste. He did not identify exact disliked voices. He asked for one more short study: a strong, predictable foundation, a recognizable main idea, related departures in the details, and occasional larger structural changes.

This version tests a steady four-on-the-floor kick, the same two-bar F bass phrase throughout active sections, a muted three-hit chord motif, and sparse brush/echo replies. The bass synthesis retains Negative Space's core. Original softer chord/noise samples replace its spring/friction palette. This is a taste hypothesis, not evidence that these are Olof's preferred instruments. Olof subsequently said the structure is closer, while remaining unsure about the instruments. He requested palette alternatives with groove and arrangement unchanged.

## Listening map

| Time | Musical role | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–7.5 s | Foundation and motif | Fixed kick/bass pattern; three chord hits over two bars establish the recurring idea. A soft brush layer enters after two bars. |
| 7.5–15 s | Variation | The foundation repeats. Open hats add a little movement; phrase-end chord replies and a brush fill add detail. |
| 15–20.625 s | Harmonic colour / tension | The motif keeps its rhythm but uses a suspended upper voicing over the same bass. The bass filter opens gradually. |
| 20.625–22.5 s | Breakdown | Kick and rhythmic bass stop for one bar. A held, slowed chord and breath remain. |
| 22.5–28.125 s | Return / drop | Familiar kick, bass and original chord voicing return; one short reply/fill develops the return. |
| 28.125–30 s | Release | Low F and the original chord resolve into their tails. |

**Groove** names the rhythmic feel; **motif** is the short recurring idea; **development** changes that idea while retaining its identity; **fills** are brief connecting gestures; **timbre** is a sound's character. A harmonic progression specifically changes chords, while arrangement and thematic development can give direction without constantly changing the bass or harmony.

The design draws on the presentation/continuation distinction in [Open Music Theory's sentence chapter](https://openmusictheory.github.io/sentence.html), and changing one meaningful parameter at a time in [Dennis DeSantis's variation exercise](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/creating-variation-2-mutation-over-generations). This techno excerpt is not presented as a formal classical sentence: it borrows the establish/develop relationship without the classical cadence requirements.

## Rendering and screening

V001's detail stem measured much lower than its other parts. V002 raises the muted motif and brush gestures, and returns the reply sample to its original pitch with a less severe high-pass filter. The actual kick/sub event schedules are identical between these revisions. No master compression or loudness boost was added.

The final master measures **−17.61 LUFS, −3.86 dBTP**, with zero PCM rail contacts. All five renders are exactly 1,440,000 frames at 48 kHz stereo. The last 20 ms peaks below −69 dBFS. The event audit verifies four evenly spaced kicks on every active bar, the same two-bar bass pattern, and unchanged motif timing across the upper-voicing change. See [evidence](evidence.json) and [audit](audit.py).

These are technical checks, not proof of musical quality or a personal listening claim. No audio-model review was used; earlier duplicate controls demonstrated unreliable subtle judgments. Taste remains for Olof to assess. The shared workbench's 36 Python tests and Ruff passed; no renderer or reusable tool code changed.

## Reproduce and preserve

From `apps/music`:

```sh
uv run --locked python music.py assemble projects/undercurrent/assembly-v002.json --out NEW_ASSEMBLY
uv run --locked python music.py render-project NEW_ASSEMBLY/project.json --revision v002 --out NEW_RENDER
```

The relative sample paths in a newly assembled recipe are generated by the assembler. Use the project manifest from its current location. [Palette generator](make_palette.py) records deterministic synthesis and hashes in [palette provenance](references/palette.json); it refuses to overwrite existing samples. The retained 909 library and its provenance remain in `../practical-dogfood/references/`. No artist recording is incorporated.

`exports/bundle-v002/` contains a verified copy of assembled source, all registered samples, the raw master and four stems. It requires the installed music workbench to render and is not an external backup. Versioned sources, samples and both renders remain preserved. Audio is local/ignored; generators, manifests and evidence are tracked.
